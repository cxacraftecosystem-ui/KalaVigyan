# The page checklist

Every craft, family, category and answer page of KalaVigyan meets these 26 rules, whoever works on
it: a contributor delivering facts, structure, components, pictures or a whole page bundle over the
MCP server, a curator deepening a page, an engineer building a screen, or a critic scoring the
result. They were learnt from the critics of the first consolidated page (2026-11-19) and the
owner's density rule (2026-11-20). Each rule is written for any craft; an example shows how one
craft met a rule and is never the rule itself. A page meets every rule before anyone scores it,
and a contribution never breaks one.

## First, derive the page's own lists

Before researching or building, write down three lists for this craft, family or category. Derive
them from what the page holds (its facts, stages, reactions and materials: `get_facts`,
`get_section` and `get_coverage` on the MCP server, or the exported dossier), never from memory.
The rules below refer to them.

- **Mechanisms**: the distinct pieces of science that make the craft what it is, one per
  transformation a reader would ask "why does that happen?" about. By family: a dyed or printed
  textile has one per colour or per mordant and dye pair; pottery and terracotta have the clay
  body, the firing atmosphere and any slip or glaze; metal has the alloy, the casting or forming,
  and the patina or inlay; stone has the rock and how it is worked; wood and lacquer have the
  timber, its seasoning and the finish; cane, bamboo and grass have the fibre and its treatment;
  painting has the ground, the pigments and the binder.
- **Ambiguous terms**: every word of the page or its kin crafts that names two different things (a
  common name for two substances, a craft word that is also a chemistry word, a local name shared
  by two plants). Examples: lime the fruit and lime the mineral; soda as sodium carbonate and
  soda-lime glass; lac the insect resin and lacquer; "black" pottery from reduction firing and black
  from a pigment.
- **Signature fact**: the one moment that defines the craft for a reader. Examples: cloth turning
  from green to blue in air; a bell-metal casting rung for its tone; clay turning red or black in
  the kiln.

## The first minute

1. **A short headline, never a raw question.** An answer page edits the reader's question into a
   display line, with the full wording behind "Show the full question"; the raw question appears
   nowhere else either, not in Cite this, metadata or titles. A craft page opens on its name, its
   native name and a one-line lede. A component's title states its finding or what it shows,
   never a question.
2. **The best photograph is in the first viewport**: the masthead picture beside the lede on
   desktop and under the name on a phone, cropped from its focal box so people and work stay in
   frame. The picture given for the masthead is the page's best; a stand-in picture says what it
   actually shows.
3. **One masthead.** One picture holds the masthead, and no card, label or component repeats the
   masthead's GI date, place or name.

## Counting and labels

4. **One count, and it is true.** Sections and chapters are named consistently and counted once,
   and every number on the page is the number its facts state: never rounded, inflated or
   inferred.
5. **Provenance counts only this page's own sentences.** Sources borrowed from the family, the
   category or kin crafts are counted and numbered separately, and a family's fact is never filed
   as the craft's own.

## Repetition

6. **The signature fact is the hero once.** It is explained where it happens (the opening figure,
   the mechanism's player or explainer); everywhere else it is a link to that figure. Alternative
   tellings (a comic, film subtitles, the student version) are the only exception.
7. **Each picture appears once, and no two pictures look alike** (same scene, person, photographer
   and object). Replace one, or crop it to a clearly different subject, credited as cropped. Look
   at the page's pictures before giving another.
8. **The media chapter is a gallery**: illustrations, video and film keyed to the section titles,
   with no text retold.
9. **One citation panel** with tabs (this answer, the monograph), not stacked blocks. A component
   cites through its units and never carries a citation list of its own.

## Complete at rest

10. **No progress state at rest**: no "Writing section n of m", percentages or "n of m scenes". The
    page and every component on it rest finished (a player on the step the page shows, a figure
    whole), and any replay is opt-in.
11. **Feedback is visible where the reader is**: toasts are fixed to the viewport, and what a
    control did is announced where the reader pressed it.
12. **Sticky bars are opaque and never overlap** each other or the chapter rail. A component draws
    no bar, toast or overlay of its own.

## Layout

13. **No dead zones.** A picture shares its row with a quote, figure or caption; a framed box is
    sized to its content; a chapter lead never floats over an empty field.
14. **A credit sits under its own figure**, after the caption, never a screen away from it.
15. **A card with no sentence is not drawn.** A kin-craft or shared-concept panel joins crafts only
    through sentences that use the term in the same sense (rule 18).
16. **The story closes before the tools**: a short coda (a practitioner's quote over the finished
    work), then the reading tools compact, then sources. Keep the page under about 47,000 px on
    desktop; past that, the last third reads as an appendix.
17. **Charts are drawn to scale**, from zero where the quantity is a depth, length or count.

## Science and chemistry

18. **Keep ambiguous terms apart.** Every term on the ambiguous list is used in one sense per
    sentence and per fact. Sentences and kin panels are never joined across senses.
19. **Every transformation says what changes and what does it**, at the level the page teaches:
    bonds, oxidation states, phase, crystal structure, moisture or temperature. It names which
    species is oxidised or reduced, what acts as mordant, flux, binder or catalyst, and what heat
    or air does. When a source's loose wording would hand a role to the wrong species, the
    sentence follows the chemistry and the quote stays as it is.
20. **Every mechanism on the list gets equal depth**: a drawing or structure plus its key step and
    a cited sentence for each. The thinnest mechanism sets the page's level, so none is left at one
    line.
21. **Equations are balanced for atoms and charge, and quantities are plausible** (units,
    magnitudes, temperatures) before anything is proposed or drawn.
22. **A source error stays verbatim and is marked**: the quote keeps the source's words, and the
    fact or sentence marks the error [sic] with the correct formula or value beside it. The same
    quote renders the same way everywhere on the page.

## Every field, densely (owner, 2026-11-20)

23. **Every field a source covers is on the page**, in the chapter where a reader looks for it:
    overview and history in the opening chapters, process and materials with the making, chemistry,
    physics and botany with the science, geography, geology, climate and ecology with the place,
    economics, society, design and policy with the people and the market, health beside the stage
    or material it concerns. An answer page draws on every field its question touches, through one
    expert seat per field, and its provenance names the fields it consulted.
24. **Each field is dense**: three or more cited sentences that each add a quantity, a mechanism, a
    name or a date, plus a figure, table, fact grid or quantity where a source gives numbers. No
    field is one sentence long.
25. **Every screen earns its height.** Margin notes, fact grids, small multiples and tables carry
    detail beside the prose instead of below it; no empty band, no decorative spacer beyond the
    spacing scale, and no card that repeats a sentence already on screen.
26. **A missing field is research, not a hole.** It never appears as an empty heading or a "not
    yet" note; it appears as a question that starts cited research, and as open work on the MCP
    server.

## How a page is checked

- A contribution is seen before it is submitted: `preview_page` draws a bundle exactly as the site
  will draw the page, behind a private link for 24 hours. Read it at 360 and 1440 px wide, in light
  and dark, against these rules; fix what breaks one and preview again before `propose_page`.
- After a page is built, one critic reads it in a browser, design and science together, at 360 and
  1440 px in light and dark, and scores it against this checklist. Everything that keeps it below 9
  is fixed, then it is scored once more. No judge panels.
- A page the owner approved is served as it was built, not rebuilt.
