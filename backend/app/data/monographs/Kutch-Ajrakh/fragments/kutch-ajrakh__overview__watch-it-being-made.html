Geography and waterWritten

Edwards writes that the River Suran served Dhamadka until the late 1980s, and the GI journal records that its water stream dried up in 1989. After the 2001 earthquake Dhamadka's water held more iron, which saddens dye colours, and even one percent of iron leaves black spots on myrobalan-dyed cloth. Ajrakhpur was chosen for good quality groundwater, but its water table is going down and excess iron and rising salinity threaten the craft again.

EconomicsWritten

Khatri printers knew their clients through hereditary relationships and bartered their dyed cloth directly with them for milk, animals and grain. Khatri printers of Kutch have almost stopped printing ajrakh for Maldharis, who buy cheaper screen-printed cloth in chemical dyes, mostly from Pakistan and from Khavda. In 2014 Abduljabbar M. Khatri's workshop made 70,000 m of running cotton yardage for Fabindia, one of his largest clients with Maiwa.

Balance, order and the priest-king's cloud motif

DesignWritten

Ajrakh design follows mizan, the Islamic principle of balance and order, with the design square divided into quarters and then sixteen parts. A fixed layout sets a large central body of a repeated motif on a tight grid within borders and end panels. Block printers identify the trefoil on the Mohenjo-daro priest-king's shawl with kakar, the cloud motif of ajrakh.
