# The fields

KalaVigyan files every fact under one primary field and up to three further fields from this
vocabulary of sixteen. A page is organised by them, and every field a source covers is on the page
(the page checklist, rules 23 to 26).

| Field | Title | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| OVERVIEW | What it is | what the craft is, where and by whom it is practised |
| PROCESS | How it is made | how the craft is made, stage by stage |
| CHEMISTRY | The chemistry | chemistry |
| PHYSICS | The physics | physics |
| MATERIALS | Materials | materials science |
| GEOGRAPHY | Geography and water | geography and water |
| GEOLOGY | Geology and soil | geology and soil |
| CLIMATE | Climate | climate |
| ECOLOGY | Ecology | ecology |
| BOTANY | Dye and fibre plants | botany of dye and fibre plants |
| HISTORY | History | history |
| ECONOMICS | Economics | economics and markets |
| DESIGN | Design | design and art history |
| HEALTH | Health and safety | health, toxicology and safe handling |
| SOCIETY | Communities and livelihoods | the communities, families and livelihoods of the craft |
| POLICY | GI, law and schemes | Geographical Indication, law and public schemes |

## What each field holds

- **OVERVIEW**: what the craft makes and what it is called, where it is practised and by which
  communities, at the depth of a page's first paragraph.
- **PROCESS**: the stages in the order a source states them, what each stage does, its tools, and
  its duration or season as quoted.
- **CHEMISTRY**: reactions and the species in them, dyes, pigments and mordants as compounds,
  oxidation and reduction, pH, and what each reagent does.
- **PHYSICS**: heat and temperature, firing and casting conditions, the mechanics of tools and
  looms, the optics of colour and lustre, sound.
- **MATERIALS**: what the work is made of and how it behaves: fibres, clays, metals and alloys,
  stone, timber, lac and resins, their grades and preparation.
- **GEOGRAPHY**: where the craft is made and why there: rivers and water and its quality, trade
  routes, distances.
- **GEOLOGY**: the rocks, clays, ores and soils the craft draws on, and where they occur.
- **CLIMATE**: the seasons of work, drying, humidity and rainfall as they shape the craft, and the
  changes a source reports.
- **ECOLOGY**: the species and habitats the materials come from, harvesting and its effects,
  effluents and waste.
- **BOTANY**: the dye, fibre and resin plants: botanical names, the part used, cultivation and
  harvest.
- **HISTORY**: origins, dated records, migrations, patronage, decline and revival.
- **ECONOMICS**: prices with their dates, markets, wages, production and exports, the size of the
  workforce.
- **DESIGN**: motifs, patterns and forms and their names, palettes, art-historical links.
- **HEALTH**: the hazards of materials and processes, toxicology, safe handling, occupational
  health studies of the craft.
- **SOCIETY**: the communities and families of the craft, practitioners a published source names,
  apprenticeship, livelihoods and who does which work.
- **POLICY**: the Geographical Indication and its record, laws and standards, public schemes and
  their figures.

## Filing

- A fact is filed under the field it explains: a mordant's chemistry is CHEMISTRY even when it sits
  on a stage.
- Further fields name the other fields the same fact explains, each once and never the primary one.
- Structure counts for its field: a community and the facts it rests on count for SOCIETY, the GI
  record and its facts for POLICY.
- A climate, geography or geology fact is what a source states about the craft; values measured by
  district belong to the datasets (the dataset guide, `datasets.md`).
